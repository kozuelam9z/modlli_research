## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): Here I have to judge whether the empirical work behind a proposed automated appearance measure would satisfy a careful referee in measurement science and fairness evaluation. For this kind of contribution, that has three parts. First, the instrument: is the construct defined clearly, is the image-to-colour pipeline fully specified, and is there evidence that the measure is reliable and valid? Second, the audits that use the instrument: are disaggregated comparisons reported with honest uncertainty, multiplicity control, and attention to confounders? Third, any causal claims built on synthetic or generative interventions: do they show that the intervention changed what it was meant to change and nothing else? Another lab should be able to rerun the pipeline from what is documented. Under the contract's failure rules, a fatal finding here ends in rejection, and either a block or a warn here forces major revision. That gives this dimension the most weight in the panel outcome, so my triggers need to be calibrated rather than reflexive.

D2 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): This dimension covers how well the manuscript is organised and explained and whether it follows the conventions a venue expects for methods papers. I am not eligible to score it. From a methodology standpoint, it still affects my work. If the pipeline, definitions, and analysis choices are not laid out clearly, I cannot verify rigor, so any methodology problem caused only by unclear exposition gets passed to the editor-in-chief seat. I do not score it under D1. A warn or worse here leads to minor revision at most.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: (a) Construct: an explicit statement of what is measured (colour as rendered in a photograph, as opposed to constitutive pigmentation, self-identified race or ethnicity, or categorical skin-type scales), with a reason for each colour dimension retained (for example lightness versus hue angle in CIELAB) and evidence that the extra dimensions carry information beyond a lightness-only or single-angle index. (b) Pipeline transparency: how skin pixels are selected or segmented; how highlights, shadows, makeup, hair and occlusions are handled; colour-space conversion assumptions (sRGB transfer function, linearization, reference white and chromatic adaptation); the aggregation statistic; and every threshold and parameter. (c) Reliability: agreement across repeated images of the same person, crops, segmenters, compression levels and resolutions, reported as ICC or limits of agreement with intervals. (d) Validity: convergent evidence against an external anchor, either multi-rater human perceptual judgments with inter-rater agreement and rater composition reported, or instrument readings (colorimeter or spectrophotometer) or controlled-capture images with a colour target; discriminant evidence that the measure is not mainly tracking illumination, white balance or camera; and a sensitivity analysis over controlled illuminant and white-balance shifts that quantifies error in perceptual units such as delta-E. (e) Fairness audits: per-group sample sizes; interval estimates for every disaggregated metric, clustered by subject or identity where images repeat; effect sizes as well as significance tests; correction across the group-by-metric-by-model family; continuous or regression analyses alongside any binning, with sensitivity to bin edges; and adjustment or stratification for image-condition confounders (lighting, pose, blur, resolution, source dataset) and correlated demographics. (f) Counterfactual or generative interventions: a manipulation check showing the targeted colour dimension moved by a measured amount; off-target checks showing identity, pose, background, lighting, texture and the other colour dimension stayed fixed (identity-embedding similarity, measured drift, human verification); dose-response across intervention magnitudes; null or sham edits to separate generator artifacts from colour effects; and attribution of model-output change to the intended dimension. (g) Reproducibility: code, parameters, seeds, model checkpoints and prompts, dataset versions and splits, plus consent and licensing status of the face data.
what_triggers_block: The design can answer the question, but a required analysis or control is missing and can be supplied by reanalysing data the authors already hold, adding controls, or documenting. Examples: disaggregated metrics with no intervals or with intervals that ignore clustering by subject; no multiplicity handling across many group comparisons; arbitrary bins with no sensitivity to bin edges; reliability or validity reported only as point estimates, or on only one of several available datasets; image-condition confounders that are recorded or estimable but never adjusted for or stratified on; generated counterfactuals whose off-target drift or manipulation size could be computed on the existing outputs but is not reported; incremental value over single-scalar baselines claimed but not tested; human-annotation agreement or colour-conversion and segmentation parameters left undocumented.
what_triggers_warn: The core evidence is present and adequate, but reporting or robustness falls short. Examples: no per-group counts next to the metrics; differences given in raw units with no perceptual interpretation; robustness tested over only a narrow band of illumination, camera or compression perturbations; no ablation of the skin-segmentation choice; little discussion of unknown in-camera processing for in-the-wild images; rater composition or annotation protocol only partly described; generator artifacts or distribution shift acknowledged but not quantified; causal wording a little stronger than the intervention evidence supports; code, data or prompt release statements incomplete.
what_triggers_fatal: The study as designed cannot answer its own question, and new data or a redesign is needed. Examples: the measure has no external anchor at all (no human perceptual ratings, no instrument ground truth, no controlled-capture calibration), so what it captures cannot be established; available evidence shows the measure is dominated by illumination or camera variation, so between-image differences cannot be attributed to skin; causal claims about model sensitivity rest on interventions that change identity or other attributes together with colour, with no design that separates them; groups in the audit are defined by the measure under evaluation in a way that makes the reported disparity circular; or the evaluation data barely cover a range of a colour dimension the conclusions depend on, so claims about that range are not supported by observations.
